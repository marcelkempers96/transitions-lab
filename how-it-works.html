How It Works

From decision to actionable evidence, in weeks. Every engagement runs the same five stages.

"Start from a decision. Work from the human side. Report what the evidence says, including the inconvenient parts. That is the value of an independent research team."

Marcel Kempers, Director
01
Scope

Start from the decision

One design conversation nails the real question, the population that holds the answer, and the evidence that would change your mind. Most research is wasted here. We would rather get the question right.

Where the question is about a niche technology inside an entrenched regime, the BReW framework enters at this stage.

Related BReW framework What we do Who we serve
02
Design

Build the instrument

Interviews where depth matters. Surveys where comparability matters. Baseline, midline, endline where the claim is about change. Validated instruments where they exist; bespoke ones where they do not.

Grounded in the interview guide; tracked through the impact-tracking template.

Related Interview guide Impact-tracking template TRL & SRL
03
Collect

Reach the people, properly

Trained researchers who already speak the language and know the place. Informed, recorded, revocable consent. Anonymised by default. This is the stage where most studies quietly fail.

Every study is held to international ethics standards.

04
Analyse

Read the evidence honestly

Clean, code and analyse to the standard of the Lab's own published articles. Where a benchmark exists, we place your findings against it. Where none exists, we say so.

Reach, depth and experience structure the reading. See Impact Measurement.

Related Impact measurement Transitions matrix Articles
05
Deliver

Evidence you can act on

Clean data, clear analysis, a decision-maker briefing benchmarked where possible and honest about the limits. Turnaround in weeks.

The instrument is left in a form that can be re-run, so month twelve is more valuable than month one.

The five stages in practice

Four worked examples from the Lab's field engagements. Each steps through the same scope → design → collect → analyse → deliver sequence. Click a card for the full case.

E-mobility · Nairobi

Electric transport, Nairobi

A manufacturer weighing whether to expand electric two-wheelers.

  1. 01 ScopeThe real question is "does the daily economics work well enough for adoption to hold once the subsidy ends," not "are riders satisfied."
  2. 02 DesignA short survey on running costs and switching intent, paired with in-depth interviews.
  3. 03 CollectLocal researchers who ride the same routes reach riders directly, in their own language.
  4. 04 AnalyseRunning costs read against the Nairobi field engagement; qualitative interviews explain the numbers.
  5. 05 DeliverA segmented read on where adoption is real, where it is fragile, and what would have to change — plus the instrument to re-run next quarter.

Water transparency · Nairobi

MiMaji · Open water pricing

A civic-tech venture asking whether open pricing data actually reaches the households paying most.

  1. 01 ScopeNot "is the map accurate," but "does a household on the wrong side of the price gap ever see it, and does seeing it change what they do."
  2. 02 DesignIn-depth interviews with kiosk operators and residents, paired with price observations at the tap.
  3. 03 CollectEnumerators from the settlement, working in-language, reach households the platform's own analytics never sees.
  4. 04 AnalyseAwareness, trust and behaviour change coded across the sample; the price data read against what residents said they paid.
  5. 05 DeliverA reading of who the tool reaches, who it does not, and where the transparency claim holds.

Agriculture · Lombok

Pyropower · Smallholder biochar

An open-source clean-energy venture asking which of three values — heat, soil or carbon income — actually pulls adoption on a small farm.

  1. 01 ScopeWhich household economics carry adoption once the demo team has left, and who in the community can build and run the kiln themselves.
  2. 02 DesignIn-depth farmer interviews, kiln-operator observation, and a household-level tracking sheet across seasons.
  3. 03 CollectBahasa-speaking researchers with agronomy training reach farmers on their own smallholdings, at their own pace.
  4. 04 AnalyseAdoption traced through household economics, labour hours and yield rather than tonnes of biochar produced.
  5. 05 DeliverA reading of what earns Tumbuh its local name, and what would have to be true for the carbon-market promise to reach the farmer.
Open the Pyropower case →

Ecosystems · Lombok

Reef Support · Community reef monitoring

A marine-tech partner asking whether community rangers, sensors and satellite data can be braided into a single trustworthy picture of reef health.

  1. 01 ScopeWhether the rangers own the data, whether the community trusts the numbers, and whether the reef ends up better protected because of it.
  2. 02 DesignRanger interviews, community-meeting observation, and a shared indicator framework that survives cross-checking between three data sources.
  3. 03 CollectIndonesian researchers work alongside the community rangers and the Niru Foundation, in-language and on-water.
  4. 04 AnalyseData ownership, trust and management response read across ranger, satellite and sensor sources.
  5. 05 DeliverA reading of when the three-source picture holds up and when it does not, and what changes on the reef when the rangers own the record.

What the Lab does, in one line

Turning macro insights into micro decisions.

Headline figures travel; decisions sit on specific desks. The job is to translate the first into the second. A share of world refining becomes a plant siting choice. An adoption curve becomes a loan term. A permit statistic becomes a sequencing decision. Four examples from the Lab's own work.

Industrial policy · E-mobility

Macro. About 95% of LFP cathode manufacturing sits in China, as the Mineral Flows Map shows. Every new European or Brazilian EV plant with LFP cells still routes its chemistry through East Asia.

Micro. A European OEM pricing a Hungarian line knows the chain geometry before it commits capex and secures its LFP offtake on a three-year horizon rather than at spot. See China ships the factory, not just the car.

E-mobility · Field evidence

Macro. Nairobi riders, mechanics, lenders and advocates all agree an electric motorcycle is cheaper to run than petrol; nothing else about the transition is settled, as The Saving Is Agreed reports from twenty-three interviews.

Micro. A lender sizing its two-wheeler credit book writes the loan around the agreed saving and prices the real contested variables (uptime, swap availability, resale) as the risk line, not the central case.

Industrial policy · Europe

Macro. Europe measures mineral-project permits application-to-decision. The Permit Is Not the Project shows the number that decides whether the plant gets built is decision-to-operation, and nobody publishes it.

Micro. A project team in Portugal sequences community engagement, hydrology work and offtake negotiation before the permit application rather than after, so decision-to-operation does not become the new chokepoint.

Agriculture · Market design

Macro. A farmer produces six outputs; one has a price. The other five beneficiaries are not on the invoice. See Who Buys the Outcome? for the Commission's buyers-club framing.

Micro. A buyers-club platform designs its contract around the four unpriced outputs, not the one priced one, and commissions the field measurement that shows the farmer is paid for all five.

If a decision you are weighing has a macro figure attached to it, tell us what the decision is. The scope conversation is where the translation starts.

The wider frame

The Lab's approach draws on socio-technical transitions (multi-level perspective, Geels), state capacity and industrial policy (Mazzucato, Rodrik), and applied social measurement in the OECD DAC tradition. What we add is fieldwork at the depth those frameworks call for, in the settings where it is hardest to gather.
