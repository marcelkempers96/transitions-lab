How It Works

From decision to actionable evidence, in weeks. Every engagement runs the same five stages.

"Start from a decision. Work from the human side. Report what the evidence says, including the inconvenient parts. That is the value of an independent research team."

Marcel Kempers, Director
01
Scope

Start from the decision

One design conversation nails the real question, the population that holds the answer, and the evidence that would change your mind. Most research is wasted here. We would rather get the question right.

Where the question is about a niche technology inside an entrenched regime, the BReW framework enters at this stage.

Related BReW framework What we do Who we serve
02
Design

Build the instrument

Interviews where depth matters. Surveys where comparability matters. Baseline, midline, endline where the claim is about change. Validated instruments where they exist; bespoke ones where they do not.

Grounded in the interview guide; tracked through the impact-tracking template.

Related Interview guide Impact-tracking template TRL & SRL
03
Collect

Reach the people, properly

Trained researchers who already speak the language and know the place. Informed, recorded, revocable consent. Anonymised by default. This is the stage where most studies quietly fail.

Every study is held to international ethics standards.

Related Research ethics Field research Nairobi electric transport MiMaji Pyropower Reef Support
04
Analyse

Read the evidence honestly

Clean, code and analyse to the standard of the Lab's own published articles. Where a benchmark exists, we place your findings against it. Where none exists, we say so.

Reach, depth and experience structure the reading. See Impact Measurement.

Related Impact measurement Transitions matrix Articles
05
Deliver

Evidence you can act on

Clean data, clear analysis, a decision-maker briefing benchmarked where possible and honest about the limits. Turnaround in weeks.

The instrument is left in a form that can be re-run, so month twelve is more valuable than month one.

Related Monitoring & evaluation European impact tracking All case studies

Throughout, you can expect

  • Independence from the thing being measured.
  • Rigour equal to the Lab's own published research.
  • Findings written to be used by a decision-maker: plain, specific, tied to the choice in front of you.

Based in Delft, The Netherlands. Fieldwork through researchers and partners who already speak the language and know the place.

A chokepoint in East Asia.

What a European OEM does with it: secures LFP offtake on a three-year horizon rather than at spot, before signing capex on a Hungarian line.

Field-verified unit economics

One number everyone in Nairobi agrees on.

Riders, mechanics, lenders and advocates agree the electric motorcycle is cheaper to run than petrol. Nothing else about the transition is settled.

What a two-wheeler lender does with it: writes the loan around the agreed saving; prices the contested variables (uptime, swap availability, resale) as the risk line, not the central case.

The Saving Is Agreed →

Policy measurement mismatch

Europe is measuring the wrong half of the permit.

The statistic published is application-to-decision. The number that decides whether the plant gets built is decision-to-operation.

What a project team in Portugal does with it: sequences community engagement, hydrology and offtake negotiation before the application, so decision-to-operation does not become the new chokepoint.

The Permit Is Not the Project →

Missing market

A farmer produces six outputs. One has a price.

The other five beneficiaries, from water utilities to insurers, are not on the invoice.

What a buyers-club platform does with it: designs the contract around the four unpriced outputs and commissions the field measurement that proves the farmer is paid for all five.

The wider frame

The Lab's approach draws on socio-technical transitions (multi-level perspective, Geels), state capacity and industrial policy (Mazzucato, Rodrik), and applied social measurement in the OECD DAC tradition. What we add is fieldwork at the depth those frameworks call for, in the settings where it is hardest to gather.
