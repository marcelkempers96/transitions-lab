Process

How It Works

From the decision you need to make to evidence you can act on, in weeks, not seasons.

The Lab runs every engagement through the same four steps. The structure is deliberately simple, because the value is in how well each step is done, not in the complexity of the process. We start from a decision, not from data, and we work from the human side first throughout.

Start from the decision, not the data

We begin with the decision you actually face, whether to expand into a market, whether a programme is working, what to fix first, not the dataset you imagine you want. A short design conversation establishes the real question, the population that holds the answer, and the evidence that would genuinely change your mind.

Most research is wasted here, by gathering data that was never going to settle anything. We would rather spend the time getting the question right.

Build the instrument

We design the study to fit the question, interviews where depth matters, surveys where comparability matters, baseline-and-endline where change is the claim. We draw on validated instruments where they exist and build bespoke ones where they do not, always blending the impact questions with the operational and experiential ones that make findings useful day to day.

This is where the social-and-technical combination earns its keep: the instrument has to understand both the people and the system they are using.

Reach the people, properly

Trained researchers who speak the language and know the place reach respondents directly, by phone, face-to-face, or on site. We work only with participants who have given informed, recorded consent, and we manage sampling, translation, and quality control end to end. In difficult contexts this step is where most studies quietly fail; it is where the Lab's fieldcraft matters most.

Evidence you can act on

You receive clean data, a clear analysis, and a written reading of what it means, benchmarked against comparable work where a benchmark exists, and honest about the limits of what the study can conclude. Turnaround is measured in weeks. Instruments are built for repetition, so the first study can become an ongoing time series rather than a one-off.

We report what the evidence says, including the parts that are inconvenient. That is the entire value of asking an independent research organisation.

Independent, rigorous, usable

Independence from the thing being measured. Rigour equal to the Lab's own published research. And findings written to be used by a decision-maker without a translator, plain, specific, and tied to the choice in front of you.

What it looks like on a real question

Take a manufacturer weighing whether to expand electric two-wheelers into a new region. Scope: we agree the real decision is not "are riders satisfied" but "does the daily economics work well enough for adoption to hold once the subsidy ends", and we identify the rider segments that would answer it. Design: we build a short repeatable survey on running costs and switching intent, paired with a smaller set of in-depth rider interviews to reach the reasoning underneath the numbers — see the interview guide for the depth-technique behind that. Collect: local researchers who ride the same routes reach riders directly, with consent, in their own language (Field Research). Deliver: within weeks the manufacturer has a segmented read on where adoption is real, where it is fragile, and what would have to change to hold it, along with the instrument to run the same measurement again next quarter. One question, answered in a way a decision can rest on. The Roam case study shows this shape of engagement in a real market.
