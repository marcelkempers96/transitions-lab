How It Works

From the decision you need to settle to evidence you can act on, in weeks. Every Lab engagement runs the same five stages.

We start from a decision, not a dataset. We work from the human side first. And we report what the evidence says, including the parts that are inconvenient. That is the value of asking an independent research team.

01
Scope

Start from the decision

A short design conversation establishes the real question, the population that holds the answer, and the evidence that would genuinely change your mind. Most research is wasted here, by gathering data that was never going to settle anything. We would rather spend the time getting the question right.

Where the question is about how a niche technology engages the regime around it, the BRW framework enters at this stage.

02
Design

Build the instrument

Interviews where depth matters. Surveys where comparability matters. Baseline, midline and endline where the claim is about change. We use validated instruments where they exist and build bespoke ones where they do not.

Every qualitative instrument is grounded in the interview guide. For change over time, the impact-tracking template keeps baseline and endline comparable.

Related Interview guide Impact-tracking template TRL & SRL
03
Collect

Reach the people, properly

Trained researchers who speak the language and know the place reach respondents directly, by phone, face-to-face or on site. Informed, recorded, revocable consent. Anonymised by default. In low-connectivity, multilingual and dispersed settings this stage is where most studies quietly fail, and where field research craft matters most.

Ethics standards we work to
  • The Belmont Report - respect, beneficence, justice.
  • WMA Declaration of Helsinki - informed consent.
  • Innovations for Poverty Action and J-PAL - data integrity, IRB oversight.
  • EU GDPR - European personal-data handling.
  • ESRC Framework for Research Ethics - qualitative fieldwork.

04
Analyse

Read the evidence honestly

We clean, code and analyse with the same rigour as the Lab's published articles. Where a comparable benchmark exists, we place your findings against it so a number carries its comparison. Where no benchmark exists, we say so.

Reach, depth and experience structure the reading. See Impact Measurement.

05
Deliver

Evidence you can act on

Clean data, a clear analysis, and a written reading of what it means. A decision-maker briefing, benchmarked where a benchmark exists, honest about the limits. Turnaround in weeks, not seasons. The instrument is delivered in a form that can be re-run.

Because instruments are built for repetition, the first study can become an ongoing time series. That continuity is what makes evidence more valuable in month twelve than in month one.

Throughout, you can expect

  • Independence from the thing being measured.
  • Rigour equal to the Lab's own published research.
  • Findings written to be used by a decision-maker without a translator: plain, specific, tied to the choice in front of you.

We are based in Delft, The Netherlands, and do fieldwork through researchers and partners who already speak the language and know the place.

The five stages in practice

A manufacturer weighing whether to expand electric two-wheelers into a new region.

  • 01 Scope. The real question is "does the daily economics work well enough for adoption to hold once the subsidy ends," not "are riders satisfied."
  • 02 Design. A short survey on running costs and switching intent, paired with in-depth interviews.
  • 03 Collect. Local researchers who ride the same routes reach riders directly, in their own language.
  • 04 Analyse. Running costs read against comparable data from the Roam engagement. Qualitative interviews explain the numbers.
  • 05 Deliver. Within weeks: a segmented read on where adoption is real, where it is fragile, and what would have to change. Plus the instrument to run the same measurement next quarter.

For the same method in very different contexts, see the MiMaji case in Nairobi and the St. Eustatius case in the Dutch Caribbean.

The wider frame

The Lab's approach draws on socio-technical transitions (multi-level perspective, Geels), state capacity and industrial policy (Mazzucato, Rodrik), and applied social measurement in the OECD DAC tradition. What we add is fieldwork done at the depth those frameworks call for, in the settings where it is hardest to gather.
