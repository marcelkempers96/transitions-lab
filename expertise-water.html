Water & Sanitation

How safe water and sanitation are delivered, sustained, and trusted, in the one region where the number of people without access is still rising.

Water transparency in Nairobi

Open data and community accountability change who can hold water systems to account, with the MiMaji Foundation.

Water is where the Lab's roots run deepest. Based in Delft, a global centre of water research, we pair genuine technical understanding of water systems with the field methods to study how they actually serve people.

Sub-Saharan Africa is the hardest case in the world.

  • Globally, 1 in 4 people (2.1 billion) still lack safely managed drinking water; 3.4 billion lack safely managed sanitation (WHO/UNICEF JMP, 2025).
  • In sub-Saharan Africa, rural access sits at just 53%, and the region is the only one where the absolute number without access has grown.
  • Women and girls bear the collection burden, many spending over 30 minutes a day fetching water.

The failure is rarely the engineering

The technical solutions mostly exist. Success depends on whether infrastructure is sustained, trusted, and maintained, and whether it reaches the rural and marginalised.

The Lab documents why a hand pump is maintained in one village and abandoned in the next, what builds or erodes trust in a source, and whether sanitation behaviour actually changes beyond the counting of latrines. That is the socio-technical layer an engineering audit never reaches, and the one that decides whether investment lasts.

Our water expertise extends to the Dutch-language contexts of Suriname and Guyana, and to transboundary water systems where the technical meets the political.

  • Why water infrastructure is sustained in some communities and abandoned in others.
  • What builds or erodes trust in a water source, and how trust shapes use.
  • How the collection burden falls on women and girls, and what genuinely shifts it.
  • Whether rural and marginalised populations are reached, or left further behind.
  • How sanitation behaviour actually changes.
  • Transboundary and climate-stressed water systems.

We draw on the Lab's water roots in Delft, our own fieldwork, and the public evidence base.

  • WHO/UNICEF JMP, Progress on Household Drinking Water and Sanitation and its 2025 report.
  • World Bank, water in Eastern and Southern Africa.
  • WHO, global WASH monitoring.
