# European Impact Tracking

*Independent before-and-after impact measurement for European projects, demonstrations, and socially innovative actions, designed with the people they affect, not reconstructed from memory at the end.*

Europe funds innovation at a scale unmatched anywhere in the world. Tens of thousands of projects, living labs, pilots, and demonstrations run at any moment, each one carrying a promise of impact and an obligation to show it. The funding is excellent. The evidence of what it actually changed, for whom, too often is not.

The Lab offers a service line built for exactly this gap: independent, lived-experience-centred impact tracking for European projects, set up at the start, run while the work is live, and closed out with a result that holds up to scrutiny.

**§ 1.1 / The problem**

## Impact tracking in European projects is harder than it should be

The difficulty is rarely a lack of goodwill. It is structural, and it shows up the same way across programmes.

**The baseline is missing.**  
Impact is a *change*, and a change needs a before. Yet measurement is frequently designed near the end, once there is a report to write, by which point the "before" can only be reconstructed from memory and rounded numbers. Without a baseline captured at the start, there is nothing rigorous to measure against.

**Methods are inconsistent and incomparable.**  
Across living labs and demonstrations, everyone measures differently, different indicators, different instruments, different definitions of the same word. The result is a pile of findings that cannot be compared across sites, work packages, or projects, even when they are asking the same underlying question.

**Output is mistaken for impact.**  
"Participants reached," "workshops delivered," "users onboarded", these are activities, not outcomes. They are easy to count and easy to report, and they tell you almost nothing about whether anyone's life or work actually changed. The harder, more meaningful dimensions get neglected because the data was never set up to capture them.

**The people most affected are rarely heard systematically.**  
Impact lands in someone's lived experience. But the citizen, the worker, the end-user is seldom asked, in a structured and repeatable way, what changed for them and what they would fix first. The most important evidence is the evidence no one designed to collect.

**Self-assessment strains credibility.**  
A project evaluating its own impact has every incentive to find success. That is not dishonesty; it is gravity. Independent measurement exists precisely to counter it, and independence is the thing that is hardest to arrange from inside a consortium that designed the intervention.

**Evidence arrives too late to use.**  
When measurement is an end-of-project exercise, the findings land after every meaningful decision has already been made. Impact data is only valuable if it can still change something, which means it has to be flowing while the project is live, not assembled once it is over.

**§ 1.2 / What we do**

## Set it up at the start, run it throughout, close it with proof

The Lab builds the impact-tracking architecture into a project from the beginning and runs it across the full life of the work, so that the final result is a genuine before-and-after rather than a reconstruction.

**Before, baseline by design.**  
At the outset we agree what impact this project is actually claiming, identify who should change and how, and capture a real baseline against which everything that follows will be measured. The "before" exists because we went and recorded it, not because someone remembers it.

**During, measurement while it matters.**  
We run structured, repeatable measurement through the live phase of the project: midline readings, lived-experience data from the people affected, and early signals of what is working and what is not, returned while there is still time to act on them. Impact tracking becomes a steering instrument, not a post-mortem.

**After, a result that holds up.**  
At close we deliver the endline measurement and the before-and-after comparison: what changed, for whom, by how much, and through what pathway, reported independently, including the parts that are inconvenient. The output is designed to satisfy a funder, survive review, and tell the project team something true.

**§ 1.3 / Templates and design support**

## A measurement framework the project can actually run

Much of the problem is solved before any data is collected, in the design. The Lab provides the framework and the instruments so that measurement is consistent, comparable, and light enough to sustain.

**Baseline and endline templates** built around the project's specific impact claims, so the before and after are measured the same way and the comparison is valid.

**During-project monitoring instruments**, short, standardised, repeatable readings designed to run mid-flight without overwhelming the team or the participants.

**Lived-experience surveys** that put structured questions to the people the project affects, built for repetition so a single survey becomes a time series.

**A shared indicator set** that lets results be compared across sites, work packages, and partners instead of fragmenting into incomparable pieces.

**Impact-pathway mapping** that makes the logic explicit, from activity to outcome to impact, so everyone can see what is being claimed and what would count as evidence for it.

Templates can be handed over for a partner to run with the Lab's support, or operated by the Lab as the independent measurement function within the project. Either way, the design is consistent enough that one project's evidence can stand beside another's.

**§ 1.4 / What we measure**

## Reach, depth, and experience, for the people a project serves

We measure **reach**, who is actually being affected, and who is being left out. We measure **depth**, how materially lives, work, or systems change, beyond the headcount. And we measure **experience**, what the change feels like to the people inside it, the friction they encounter, and the thing they would fix first.

Because the instruments are standardised and repeatable, a project's measurement does not end as a single number. It becomes a trajectory that can be managed, compared, and defended.

**§ 1.5 / How we work with European projects**

## As the independent measurement partner

The Lab is built to sit inside European projects as the independent impact function, the partner or work-package lead responsible for measurement, precisely because it did not design the intervention.

We work with **project consortia** needing a credible, independent partner to own impact measurement from baseline to endline. With **funders and programme bodies** wanting comparable impact evidence across a portfolio of projects and demonstrations. With **public-sector and regional bodies** running socially innovative actions that have to show real change to the people they serve. And with **living labs and demonstration sites** needing measurement consistent enough to compare across locations.

---

**§ 1.6 / Why the Lab**

## Independence is the whole point

The reason to bring in the Lab is the reason a project cannot do this convincingly itself. We are independent of the intervention we are measuring. We are social and technical at once, we start from the people a project affects, and we also understand the system being demonstrated. And we report what the data says, including when it is inconvenient.

That independence is not a marketing line. It is the source of the evidence's value. A result the Lab reports means something a self-assessment cannot.
