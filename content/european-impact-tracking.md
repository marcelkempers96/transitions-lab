The Lab offers a service line built for exactly this gap: independent impact tracking, set up at the start, run while the work is live, closed out with a result that holds up to scrutiny.

## Impact tracking in European projects is harder than it should be

The difficulty is rarely a lack of goodwill. It is structural, and it shows up the same way across programmes.

**The baseline is missing.**
Impact is a *change*, and a change needs a before. Yet measurement is frequently designed near the end, once there is a report to write, by which point the "before" can only be reconstructed from memory and rounded numbers. Without a baseline captured at the start, there is nothing rigorous to measure against. See our [impact-tracking template](/impact-tracking-template) for a fill-in framework that fixes this at project design.

**Methods are inconsistent and incomparable.**
Across living labs and demonstrations, everyone measures differently: different indicators, different instruments, different definitions of the same word. The result is a pile of findings that cannot be compared across sites, work packages, or projects, even when they are asking the same underlying question. The [European Commission's Better Regulation guidelines](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-making-process/planning-and-proposing-law/better-regulation_en) call for comparability; achieving it takes disciplined instrument design from day one.

**Output is mistaken for impact.**
"Participants reached," "workshops delivered," "users onboarded" - these are activities, not outcomes. They are easy to count and easy to report, and they tell you almost nothing about whether anyone's life or work actually changed. The [OECD DAC evaluation criteria](https://www.oecd.org/dac/evaluation/daccriteriaforevaluatingdevelopmentassistance.htm) separate output from outcome and outcome from impact; European reporting frameworks like the [Horizon Europe Key Impact Pathways](https://research-and-innovation.ec.europa.eu/strategy/support-policy-making/support-eu-research-and-innovation-policy-making/evaluation-impact-assessment-and-monitoring/horizon-europe-key-impact-pathways_en) build the same distinction in.

**The people most affected are rarely heard systematically.**
Impact lands in someone's lived experience. But the citizen, the worker, the end-user is seldom asked, in a structured and repeatable way, what changed for them and what they would fix first. Our [in-depth interview guide](/interview-guide) documents the five-levels-down principle behind the Lab's qualitative reach; the [Roam](/case-roam) and [MiMaji](/case-mimaji) cases show that reach in practice.

**Self-assessment strains credibility.**
A project evaluating its own impact has every incentive to find success. That is not dishonesty; it is gravity. Independent measurement exists precisely to counter it, and independence is the thing that is hardest to arrange from inside a consortium that designed the intervention.

**Evidence arrives too late to use.**
When measurement is an end-of-project exercise, the findings land after every meaningful decision has already been made. Impact data is only valuable if it can still change something, which means it has to be flowing while the project is live, not assembled once it is over.

## Set it up at the start, run it throughout, close it with proof

The Lab builds the impact-tracking architecture into a project from the beginning and runs it across the full life of the work, so that the final result is a genuine before-and-after rather than a reconstruction. This is the [five-stage method](/how-it-works) applied to a European project timeline.

**Before, baseline by design.**
At the outset we agree what impact this project is actually claiming, identify who should change and how, and capture a real baseline against which everything that follows will be measured. The "before" exists because we went and recorded it, not because someone remembers it.

**During, measurement while it matters.**
We run structured, repeatable measurement through the live phase of the project: midline readings, lived-experience data from the people affected, and early signals of what is working and what is not, returned while there is still time to act on them. Impact tracking becomes a steering instrument, not a post-mortem. See [Monitoring, Evaluation & Dissemination](/monitoring-evaluation-dissemination) for the continuing shape of that work.

**After, a result that holds up.**
At close we deliver the endline measurement and the before-and-after comparison: what changed, for whom, by how much, and through what pathway, reported independently, including the parts that are inconvenient. The output is designed to satisfy a funder, survive review, and tell the project team something true.

## The European programmes we work with

Impact tracking is not one thing; every European instrument carries its own reporting logic. The Lab has designed measurement architecture for projects funded under, or comparable to, the following:

- **[Horizon Europe](https://research-and-innovation.ec.europa.eu/funding/funding-opportunities/funding-programmes-and-open-calls/horizon-europe_en)** - research and innovation actions, Coordination and Support Actions, and the [Missions](https://research-and-innovation.ec.europa.eu/funding/funding-opportunities/funding-programmes-and-open-calls/horizon-europe/eu-missions-horizon-europe_en) on climate, cancer, oceans, cities, and soil. Impact tracked against [Key Impact Pathways](https://research-and-innovation.ec.europa.eu/strategy/support-policy-making/support-eu-research-and-innovation-policy-making/evaluation-impact-assessment-and-monitoring/horizon-europe-key-impact-pathways_en).
- **[European Social Fund Plus (ESF+)](https://ec.europa.eu/european-social-fund-plus/en)** and its [Social Innovation+ strand](https://ec.europa.eu/european-social-fund-plus/en/social-innovation) - socially innovative actions, transferability across Member States, and the specific reporting we've written about in the [ESF+ Social Innovation article](/esf-social-innovation).
- **[LIFE programme](https://cinea.ec.europa.eu/programmes/life_en)** - climate, environment, and circular-economy demonstrations, where before-and-after evidence at the demonstration site is the whole point.
- **[Just Transition Fund](https://ec.europa.eu/regional_policy/funding/just-transition-fund_en)** - territorial transition plans that promise both economic diversification and social protection; both need honest measurement.
- **[European Innovation Council (EIC)](https://eic.ec.europa.eu/)** - Pathfinder, Transition, and Accelerator projects taking breakthrough tech from lab to market. See our reading of the innovation-to-scale gap in [Europe Invents, America Scales](/insight-eu-us).
- **[Interreg](https://interreg.eu/)** - cross-border, transnational, and interregional cooperation programmes where comparability across sites is a first-order requirement.
- **[Digital Europe Programme](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/activities/digital-programme)** - European Digital Innovation Hubs, digital public services, and AI deployment where the measurable change is behavioural, not technical.
- **[EIT Knowledge and Innovation Communities](https://eit.europa.eu/our-communities/eit-innovation-communities)** - climate, urban mobility, food, health, digital, manufacturing, raw materials - the eight KICs that connect research, business, and education across Europe.
- **[New European Bauhaus](https://new-european-bauhaus.europa.eu/)** - community pilots at the intersection of sustainability, aesthetics, and inclusion, where lived experience is the metric.
- **[Cohesion Policy](https://ec.europa.eu/regional_policy/funding_en)** and **[Interreg Europe](https://www.interregeurope.eu/)** - regional development, urban regeneration, and territorial cooperation where change happens at multiple scales at once.
- **National and regional programmes** with an EU co-funding component - [Recovery and Resilience Facility](https://commission.europa.eu/business-economy-euro/economic-recovery/recovery-and-resilience-facility_en) national plans, ERDF actions, and Member State partnerships tied to European targets.

Wherever the work sits, the same discipline applies: [start from the decision](/how-it-works), reach the people, report what the evidence says.

## A measurement framework the project can actually run

Much of the problem is solved before any data is collected, in the design. The Lab provides the framework and the instruments so that measurement is consistent, comparable, and light enough to sustain. See [Resources](/resources) for the templates and methods available openly.

**Baseline and endline templates** built around the project's specific impact claims, so the before and after are measured the same way and the comparison is valid. Our [impact-tracking template](/impact-tracking-template) is the openly-published version.

**During-project monitoring instruments** - short, standardised, repeatable readings designed to run mid-flight without overwhelming the team or the participants.

**Lived-experience surveys** that put structured questions to the people the project affects, built for repetition so a single survey becomes a time series. Grounded in the [in-depth interview guide](/interview-guide).

**A shared indicator set** that lets results be compared across sites, work packages, and partners instead of fragmenting into incomparable pieces.

**Impact-pathway mapping** that makes the logic explicit - from activity to outcome to impact - so everyone can see what is being claimed and what would count as evidence for it. For niche technologies engaging incumbent regimes, this mapping benefits from the [BRW framework](/brw), which names the mechanism the project is deploying against a specific barrier.

Templates can be handed over for a partner to run with the Lab's support, or operated by the Lab as the independent measurement function within the project. Either way, the design is consistent enough that one project's evidence can stand beside another's.

## Reach, depth, and experience - for the people a project serves

We measure **reach**: who is actually being affected, and who is being left out. We measure **depth**: how materially lives, work, or systems change, beyond the headcount. And we measure **experience**: what the change feels like to the people inside it, the friction they encounter, and the thing they would fix first. This three-dimensional reading is elaborated on the [Impact Measurement](/impact-measurement) page and draws on the framing developed by [60 Decibels](https://60decibels.com/) for lived-impact reporting.

Because the instruments are standardised and repeatable, a project's measurement does not end as a single number. It becomes a trajectory that can be managed, compared, and defended.

## How we work with European projects

The Lab is built to sit inside European projects as the independent impact function - the partner or work-package lead responsible for measurement, precisely because it did not design the intervention.

We work with:

- **[Project consortia](/who-we-serve)** needing a credible, independent partner to own impact measurement from baseline to endline.
- **Funders and programme bodies** - for example, [DG RTD](https://commission.europa.eu/about-european-commission/departments-and-executive-agencies/research-and-innovation_en), [DG EMPL](https://commission.europa.eu/about-european-commission/departments-and-executive-agencies/employment-social-affairs-and-inclusion_en), [DG CLIMA](https://commission.europa.eu/about-european-commission/departments-and-executive-agencies/climate-action_en), and their executive agencies like [CINEA](https://cinea.ec.europa.eu/) and [EISMEA](https://eismea.ec.europa.eu/) - wanting comparable impact evidence across a portfolio of projects and demonstrations.
- **Public-sector and regional bodies** running socially innovative actions that have to show real change to the people they serve.
- **Living labs and demonstration sites** - including those in the [European Network of Living Labs (ENoLL)](https://enoll.org/) - needing measurement consistent enough to compare across locations.

## Independence is the whole point

The reason to bring in the Lab is the reason a project cannot do this convincingly itself. We are independent of the intervention we are measuring. We are [social and technical at once](/about) - we start from the people a project affects, and we also understand the system being demonstrated. And we report what the data says, including when it is inconvenient. Our published [articles](/articles) and [articles](/articles) are the proof of that method; the same discipline is what a commissioning consortium receives.

That independence is not a marketing line. It is the source of the evidence's value. A result the Lab reports means something a self-assessment cannot.
