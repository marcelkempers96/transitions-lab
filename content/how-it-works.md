# How It Works

*From the decision you need to settle to evidence you can act on, in weeks, not seasons. Every Lab engagement runs the same five stages. The structure is deliberately simple; the value is in how well each stage is done.*

We start from a decision, never from a dataset we happen to have. We work from the human side first throughout. And we report what the evidence says, including the parts that are inconvenient. That is the entire value of asking an [independent research team](/about) rather than an instrument built to reassure.

<div class="stage">
  <div class="stage-num">01</div>
  <span class="stage-eyebrow">Scope</span>
  <h2>Start from the decision, not the data</h2>
  <p>We begin with the choice you actually face. Whether to expand into a market. Whether a programme is working. What to fix first. What has to change before you can act. A short design conversation establishes the real question, the population that holds the answer, and the evidence that would genuinely change your mind.</p>
  <p>Most research is wasted here, by gathering data that was never going to settle anything. We would rather spend the time getting the question right. When the question is about how a niche technology will engage the regime around it, the <a href="/brw">BRW framework</a> enters at this stage, so the study measures the right mechanism (bypass, repurpose, or weaken) rather than a generic outcome.</p>

  <h2>Build the instrument</h2>
  <p>We design the study to fit the question. Interviews where depth matters. Surveys where comparability matters. Baseline, midline, and endline where the claim is about change.   <div class="stage-num">03</div>
  <span class="stage-eyebrow">Collect</span>
  <h2>Reach the people, properly</h2>
  <p>Trained researchers who speak the language and know the place reach respondents directly, by phone, face-to-face, or on site. We work only with participants who have given informed, recorded, revocable consent, and we anonymise by default. In difficult contexts, low-connectivity, multilingual, informal, dispersed, this stage is where most studies quietly fail; it is where the Lab's <a href="/field-research">field research</a> craft matters most.</p>
  <p>Consent and ethics standards follow independent research norms in the tradition of <a href="https://poverty-action.org/">Innovations for Poverty Action</a> and the <a href="https://www.povertyactionlab.org/">Abdul Latif Jameel Poverty Action Lab (J-PAL)</a>, adapted to the settings we actually work in. See the <a href="/case-roam">Roam</a>, <a href="/case-mimaji">MiMaji</a>, and <a href="/case-pyropower">Pyropower</a> cases for three contrasting examples of how this stage plays out on the ground.</p>

  <span class="stage-eyebrow">Deliver</span>
  <h2>Evidence you can act on</h2>
  <p>You receive clean data, a clear analysis, and a written reading of what it means, benchmarked where a benchmark exists and honest about the limits of what the study can conclude. ## What you can expect throughout

Independence from the thing being measured. Rigour equal to the Lab's own [published research](/programmes). And findings written to be used by a decision-maker without a translator, plain, specific, and tied to the choice in front of you.

We are based in Delft, The Netherlands. We do fieldwork in the places our research takes us, through researchers and partners who already speak the language and know the place, because [the quality of field research depends on it](/geographies). Our published work on the [BRW framework](/brw), on [TRL and SRL readiness](/readiness-levels), and on the [Transitions Outcomes matrix](/insight-transitions-outcomes) is the proof of the method; the same method is what a commissioning partner receives.

## The five stages in practice

Take a manufacturer weighing whether to expand electric two-wheelers into a new region.

- **01 Scope.** The real decision is not "are riders satisfied" but "does the daily economics work well enough for adoption to hold once the subsidy ends"; we identify the rider segments that hold the answer.
- **02 Design.** A short repeatable survey on running costs and switching intent, paired with a smaller set of in-depth interviews using the [interview guide](/interview-guide) to reach the reasoning underneath the numbers, is built to fit the exact question.
- **03 Collect.** Local researchers who ride the same routes reach riders directly, with consent, in their own language ([field research](/field-research)). Consent is recorded and revocable.
- **04 Analyse.** Running costs and switching intent are read against comparable data from the [Roam](/case-roam) engagement and, where relevant, [GOGLA](https://www.gogla.org/) market signals; qualitative interviews explain the numbers.
- **05 Deliver.** Within weeks the manufacturer has a segmented read on where adoption is real, where it is fragile, and what would have to change to hold it, along with the instrument to run the same measurement again next quarter.

The [Roam case study](/case-roam) shows this shape of engagement in a real market. For a very different context under the same method, see the [MiMaji water-transparency case](/case-mimaji) in Nairobi and the [St. Eustatius Transport Directorate case](/case-statia) in the Dutch Caribbean.

## Where the method sits, in the wider landscape

The Lab's approach draws on the socio-technical transitions literature (the [Sustainability Transitions Research Network](https://transitionsnetwork.org/) is the primary hub), on the [state capacity and industrial policy](https://commission.europa.eu/topics/eu-competitiveness/draghi-report_en) debate that shapes the environment new technologies land in, and on the parallel body of applied social measurement in the [OECD DAC evaluation criteria](https://www.oecd.org/dac/evaluation/daccriteriaforevaluatingdevelopmentassistance.htm) tradition. What we add is fieldwork done at the depth those frameworks call for, in the settings where it is hardest to gather.

For the underlying theory behind our reading, see the [multi-level perspective](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0048733302000628) originated by Geels and colleagues in the early 2000s; for the state-capacity side, [Mariana Mazzucato](https://marianamazzucato.com/) on the entrepreneurial state and [Dani Rodrik](https://drodrik.scholar.harvard.edu/) on industrial policy set much of the frame the Lab's applied work operates within.
