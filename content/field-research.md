# Field Research

*Rigorous primary research in the places it is hardest to do well, built around direct contact with the people a transition affects, in developing and emerging-market contexts.*

The most important evidence about a technology or a programme is usually held by the people living inside it: the rider financing an electric motorcycle, the farmer deciding whether the kiln is worth the labour, the ranger who knows the reef better than any satellite. The Lab's field research exists to reach that evidence, systematically, ethically, and to a standard that holds up.

This is the capability behind the Lab's own published work, made available to others.

**§ 1.1 / Research in developing contexts**

## Where the evidence is hardest to gather, and matters most

The Lab works in settings that defeat conventional research: low-connectivity, multilingual, informal, dispersed. These are also the settings where decisions carry the most weight and where bad evidence does the most damage. Running research well here is not a matter of translating a Western survey and hoping. It demands researchers who speak the language, know the place, and understand the working lives of the people they are talking to.

Our fieldwork is grounded in real engagements across emerging markets, electric transport in Nairobi, decentralised clean energy in Lombok, marine monitoring across coastal Indonesia, and in five years of sustained fieldwork in Kenya behind the Lab's published programmes. We do not parachute in. We work through people who are already part of the context.

**§ 1.2 / What we do**

## Mixed-method research, run properly

**Interviews and qualitative fieldwork.**  
Structured, in-depth conversations that reach past what happened to why it happened and what it means to the person. (See the [Interview Guide](https://www.transitionslab.org/interview-guide.html) for the depth we reach.)

**Surveys at scale.**  
Standardised, repeatable instruments, by phone or face-to-face, short enough to run again next quarter, rich enough to change a decision.

**Baseline, midline, and endline measurement.**  
The before-and-after spine of any impact claim, captured at the right moments rather than reconstructed at the end.

**Observation and on-site work.**  
Being present where the technology is used, because some things are only visible in the doing.

Most research fails in one of two directions: too elaborate to repeat affordably, or too thin to tell you anything you did not already assume. Our standard is the middle ground, structured enough to compare, rich enough to matter.

**§ 1.3 / How we hold the work**

## Ethics and rigour are not optional extras

We work only with participants who have given informed, recorded, revocable consent. We anonymise by default. We manage sampling, translation, and quality control end to end, and we are explicit about the limits of what a given study can conclude. We interview to understand, not to confirm, and we report the threads that contradict a client's hopes as carefully as the ones that support them.

The same rigour that goes into the Lab's published research goes into every study we run for others. That is the point of asking an independent research organisation rather than a marketing instrument.

Commissioned work belongs to the client. We deliver it as a confidential internal report, and it stays private unless the client chooses to publish or to let us feature it. The case studies on this site are those exceptions, shared with partners' agreement. The Lab publishes its *own* research programmes openly; it does not publish clients' findings.

**§ 1.4 / Who this is for**

## Anyone who needs evidence they can stand behind

**Companies expanding into new markets**, locally or internationally, who need to understand how a product actually lands with real users before committing to scale. **Funders and public bodies** needing baseline-to-endline measurement that survives review. **NGOs and programmes** needing honest evidence of what is changing on the ground. **Researchers and academic teams** needing a field partner who can collect data to a publishable standard in difficult places.
