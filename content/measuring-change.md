# Measuring Change

*What a technology, service or programme actually changes, for whom, and through what pathway. Measured with the people it is meant to serve, and reported honestly.*

Most writing about a new programme arrives ahead of the evidence. Measurement closes that gap. We put honest questions to the people the work is meant to serve, and report what they say - including when it is inconvenient.

Measuring Change is two linked disciplines: **field research** (primary contact with the people a transition affects) and **impact measurement** (the frame that turns those conversations into a defensible reading of what changed).

## Three dimensions we measure

Every study sits inside the same frame, so a result in one context can be read against another.

<div class="callout c-coral">
  <p><strong>01 · Reach.</strong> Who is actually being reached, and who is being missed. The most flattering programme can be reaching the wrong people, or the same people repeatedly, or leaving the hardest-to-reach unmoved.</p>
</div>

<div class="callout c-cobalt">
  <p><strong>02 · Depth.</strong> How much things actually change for those reached. Income, time, health, opportunity - measured in the units respondents actually use, not the funder's logframe.</p>
</div>

<div class="callout c-butter">
  <p><strong>03 · Experience.</strong> What it is like to be on the receiving end. Friction, unintended effects, and the qualitative texture that numbers cannot carry. Usually where the useful insight lives.</p>
</div>

Reach, depth and experience read together give a picture that a self-report or an output count cannot.

---

## What we track

- **Outcomes, not outputs.** "Ten workshops delivered" is an output; "the participants can now negotiate a fair price" is an outcome. Only the second earns the word impact.
- **Quality-of-life change.** Concrete shifts in income, time, health, safety, learning, autonomy.
- **Distributional effects.** Who was reached and who was excluded. Aggregate figures hide this.
- **Unintended effects.** In either direction. Often the most important findings.
- **Customer experience.** How the intervention feels to use, and what respondents ask to change first.
- **Attribution and additionality.** Whether the change happened *because of* the intervention or *alongside* it.
- **Sustainability.** Whether the benefit persists once the project ends. Only measurable by revisiting.

## How we work

<div class="callout c-butter">
  <ul>
    <li><strong>In-depth interviews.</strong> Structured conversations that reach past what happened to why. See the <a href="/interview-guide">interview guide</a>.</li>

## Ethics

Informed, recorded, revocable consent. Anonymisation by default. Sampling, translation and quality control managed end to end. We interview to understand, not to confirm. Full standards on the [Field Research](/field-research#ethics-and-rigour) page.

Commissioned work belongs to the client and stays private unless they choose to publish. The [case studies](/case-studies) here are the exceptions.
