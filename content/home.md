§ 0 / Transitions Lab

# Aligning technology with the people it is meant to serve.

*Transitions Lab is an independent research team working on the ground, building deep understanding of socio-technical interactions and transitions, so that human values and lived experience shape how technologies arrive in the world.*

**In practice: independent field research and impact measurement for technology in the real world, run by researchers who go to where the transition is actually happening.**

We go to where a transition is actually happening, the workshop, the field, the household, and we study what a technology does once real people live with it. Then we feed that evidence back to the innovators, institutions, and communities who can act on it. People first; the technology second; the place always.

**What the Lab is**
An independent research team, not a consultancy selling a flattering number and not an advocacy group with a finding to confirm. We publish our own research openly and report what the evidence says. Work commissioned by others is confidential by default; the case studies on this site are the ones partners have agreed to make public.

**What we study**
The interaction between a technology and the human system it enters, socio-technical transitions, read from the human side first.

**Where we work**
On the ground, in the contexts where evidence is hardest to gather well, from Nairobi to Lombok, across emerging markets and European projects alike.

**How we help**
Field research, impact measurement, and the design of the instruments that make a transition legible while it is still unfolding.

**§ 0.2 / The mission, stated plainly**

## Technology is not destiny. It can be steered.

The direction a transition takes is not fixed in advance. A new technology can arrive in a way that widens access or narrows it, that respects people's actual lives or overrides them, that earns trust or burns it. The difference is rarely the technology itself. It is whether anyone was paying close, honest attention to what was happening to people on the ground, early enough for it to matter.

That attention is the Lab's entire reason to exist. We are not here to celebrate innovation or to oppose it. We are here to understand it as it lands, in human terms, and to give the people steering a transition the evidence they need to steer it well. We provide the evidence; they make the decision; and done properly, the two together bend a transition toward the humans inside it.

**§ 0.3 / What we bring**

## Social science first, technical depth alongside

We bring two things to a question that rarely sit in the same room. The first is social-science fieldcraft: we know how to find out what is actually happening to people, and how to make that finding hold up to scrutiny. The second is genuine technical literacy: we understand how the systems we study actually work. The combination is what allows us to align human values with technology in context.

Most evaluators have one half. Policy consultancies understand programmes but not the technologies inside them; technical labs understand systems but not the people around them. The Lab refuses the split, and starts from the people, because a socio-technical question is a human question first.

**§ 0.5 / Work**

## Where the Lab has been on the ground

The Lab's published research and its applied work share one root: direct contact with the people a transition affects. Most commissioned work is delivered privately to the client as an internal report; the engagements below are the ones partners have agreed to share publicly.

[**Electric transport in Nairobi, Roam →**](https://www.transitionslab.org/case-roam.html)
How electric two-wheelers cross the affordability threshold in a petrol-dominated market, and what carries riders across it.

[**Biochar, clean energy, and regenerative agriculture in Lombok, Pyropower →**](https://www.transitionslab.org/case-pyropower.html)
What happens when smallholder farmers turn crop waste into energy and soil, on a decentralised, open-source kiln.

[**Rangers for marine ecosystems, Reef Support →**](https://www.transitionslab.org/case-reef-support.html)
How community rangers, sensors, and satellite data combine into a shared, trustworthy picture of reef health.

**§ 0.6 / Insights**

## How we read the landscape

The Lab publishes independent analysis of the transitions and relationships it studies, the same evidence-first posture, applied to the big picture.

[**Europe Invents, America Scales →**](https://www.transitionslab.org/insight-eu-us.html)
Europe produces world-class innovation and struggles to commercialise it; the US does the reverse. What the asymmetry means, and where independent evidence fits in the gap.

[**The EU and Africa: Opportunity, Compatibility, and the Evidence In Between →**](https://www.transitionslab.org/insight-eu-africa.html)
Europe is committing hundreds of billions to Africa, and Africa is becoming the growth market of the century. Where the two genuinely fit, and what stands between the investment and the impact.

[**Frameworks & Resources →**](https://www.transitionslab.org/resources.html)
The Lab's frameworks, methods, and tools, published openly: the BRW strategic framework, the measurement methodology, the socio-technical primer, and the field instruments.
