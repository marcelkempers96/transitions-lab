# In-Depth Interview Guide

*A demonstration of the depth the Lab reaches in qualitative fieldwork, how a single conversation moves from surface fact to the meaning, friction, and lived experience that survey data cannot reach.*

A survey tells you *what* changed. An interview, done well, tells you *why*, *how it felt*, and *what the person would do about it*. This guide shows the structure and the probing technique behind that depth. It is built to be adapted to any subject, a technology, a service, a transition, and uses a worked example for illustration.

**§ 1 / The principle of depth**

## Five levels down

Most interviews stop at level one or two. The Lab's standard is to reach level four or five on the questions that matter, because that is where the actionable insight lives.

| Level | What it captures | Example question | What a survey gets |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1, Fact | What happened | "What did you do?" | ✓ surveys reach here |
| 2, Behaviour | How they did it | "Walk me through it." | ✓ partly |
| 3, Reasoning | Why they did it that way | "What made you choose that?" | ✗ |
| 4, Meaning | What it means to them | "What did that change for you?" | ✗ |
| 5, Tension | What they'd trade, fear, or fix | "What's the part you'd never accept?" | ✗ |

The craft is not in the question list. It is in the follow-up, staying on a thread until it reaches level four or five rather than moving to the next item.

---

**§ 2 / Structure of the conversation**

## The funnel: wide, then deep, then wide again

**Opening (5 min), establish ease and consent.**
Plain-language consent, permission to record, and a genuinely easy first question. The goal is for the person to start talking in their own words before any probing begins.

> *"Before anything else, tell me a bit about an ordinary day for you, and where [the thing we're studying] fits into it."*

**Orientation (10 min), map their world.**
Establish the context the rest of the interview depends on. Who is around them, what the alternatives were, what normal looked like before.

**Core (25-35 min), go deep on two or three threads.**
This is where depth happens. Pick the threads that carry the impact claim and follow each one down the five levels. Better to reach level five on three things than level two on ten.

**Lived experience (10 min), the change, felt.**
What is materially different now; what it cost; what they would change first; what they would never give up.

**Close (5 min), the open door.**
"What haven't I asked that I should have?" routinely produces the single best quote in the interview.

**§ 3 / Probing technique**

## How to get from level two to level five

**The echo.**  
Repeat their last few words as a question. Almost no effort; remarkably effective.
> Respondent: "…so I just stopped using it after that."
> Interviewer: "You stopped using it?"

**The ladder up (meaning).**  
Ask why something matters, then why *that* matters, until you hit a value.
> "Why was the cost the thing that mattered?" → "Why does keeping the cash matter right now?" → reaches the real constraint.

**The critical incident.**  
Don't ask about the general; ask about the last specific time.
> Not "How do you usually find it?" but "Tell me about the last time you used it. What happened, start to finish?"

**The contrast.**  
Make them compare to the alternative they actually had.
> "Compared to how you did this before, what's different?"

**The silence.**  
After an answer, wait. The second thing a person says is usually truer than the first.

**The fix-it question.**  
Hands them the controls.
> "If you could change one thing about it tomorrow, what would it be, and what would that fix for you?"

**§ 4 / Worked thread (demonstration)**

## One question, taken five levels down

This shows a single thread reaching depth. *(Generic illustration, to be replaced with a real anonymised thesis excerpt.)*

> **Q (fact):** "Did you start using [the service]?"
> A: "Yeah, for a few months."
>
> **Q (behaviour):** "Walk me through how you used it."
> A: "Mostly at the start of the week, to sort out payments."
>
> **Q (reasoning):** "What made you do it at the start of the week specifically?"
> A: "That's when the money comes in. If I wait, it's gone."
>
> **Q (meaning, ladder up):** "And being able to sort it the moment the money comes in, what does that change for you?"
> A: "I'm not chasing it all week. I know where I stand."
>
> **Q (tension, fix-it):** "What's the part you'd never go back on, and the part you'd fix tomorrow?"
> A: "I'd never go back to carrying it all in cash. But the fees, every time I move money, it takes a little. That I'd fix."

> **What the thread yields that a survey could not:** the *timing* logic (income arrives, must act immediately), the *meaning* (control, "knowing where I stand"), and the *precise friction* (per-transaction fees) the person would fix first, three things a satisfaction score of "7/10" would have hidden completely.

**§ 5 / What we extract from depth**

## From transcript to evidence

Each interview is coded and synthesised so individual depth becomes a pattern across respondents.

| From the conversation | Becomes |
|---|---|
| Recurring meanings ("control," "knowing where I stand") | Themes that explain *why* impact happens |
| The fix-it answers | A ranked list of friction, in the users' own priority |
| Critical incidents | Concrete, quotable evidence of the moment of change |
| Who never reached level four | A signal of who the project is *not* working for |
| The contrasts to the old way | The genuine before-and-after, in lived terms |

> *Depth is not the opposite of rigour. Coded properly, twenty deep interviews produce findings as defensible as a survey, and they explain the survey's numbers rather than merely restating them.*

---

**§ 6 / Standards we hold**

Informed consent, recorded and revocable. Anonymisation by default. We interview to understand, not to confirm, and we report the threads that contradict the project's hopes as carefully as the ones that support them. The same rigour that goes into the Lab's published research goes into every interview we run for others.
