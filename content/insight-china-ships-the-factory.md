# China ships the factory, not just the car

*Where Chinese EV makers are actually building now, which Western plants they are buying, and how fast their share of the car market is moving outside their own.*

<p class="article-meta"><span class="article-date">8 October 2026</span> · <span class="article-reading-time">7 min read</span></p>

Tariffs are the easy story. The harder one is the map of Chinese EV production outside China. In 2026, BYD, Geely, Great Wall, Chery and SAIC are not shipping finished cars from Shenzhen into contested markets; they are opening plants in Thailand, Brazil, Hungary, Indonesia, Spain and Russia, often on the exact sites where Ford, Daimler, GM, Renault and Nissan used to build. The move is the story.

## 1. The map of the move is tighter than the headlines

BYD is the most visible, but it is not alone. Of the roughly ten overseas plants already running, BYD holds four ([Rayong in Thailand](#bib-byd-rayong), [Camaçari in Brazil](#bib-byd-camacari), [Szeged in Hungary](#bib-byd-szeged), [Subang in Indonesia](#bib-byd-subang)), Great Wall three ([Tula in Russia](#bib-gwm-tula), [Rayong in Thailand](#bib-gwm-rayong), [Iracemápolis in Brazil](#bib-gwm-iracemapolis)), Chery one (the [Ebro joint venture in Barcelona, Spain](#bib-ebro-chery)), and Geely runs its [Volvo assets](#bib-volvo-footprint) in Sweden, Belgium and South Carolina. [SAIC's first European plant](#bib-saic-galicia) is under construction in Galicia for a 2028 start. The pattern is not scattergun. Every one of these sites sits inside or next to a market where the Chinese brand already sells, and every one carries assembly capacity in the 50,000 to 200,000-vehicles-a-year range: large enough to serve a region, small enough to keep converting as demand clears tariffs.

## 2. BYD builds biggest; everyone else buys existing

BYD's [Camaçari plant in Bahia](#bib-byd-camacari) is the largest Chinese automotive investment outside Asia, with a nameplate capacity of 150,000 electric and hybrid vehicles a year, scalable to 300,000. The [Hungarian plant at Szeged](#bib-byd-szeged), in trial production since January 2026 and scheduled for full series output in Q2 2026, lands at around 200,000 vehicles a year and is the company's first "Made in Europe" line.

Everyone else is converting. Great Wall's [Iracemápolis plant](#bib-gwm-iracemapolis) was a Daimler site before 2021. Chery's Spanish partner Ebro took the [former Nissan plant in Barcelona](#bib-ebro-chery) and converted it on a 150-million-euro bill, 60% Ebro, 40% Chery. [Great Wall's Rayong line](#bib-gwm-rayong) is a former GM plant. Geely has gone further and bought a [26.4% stake in Renault Brasil](#bib-geely-renault), part of a R$3.8 billion joint investment at the Ayrton Senna complex in São José dos Pinhais. The pattern is efficient: the car is new, the paint shop and the stamping line are not.

## 3. The market share numbers show why

Europe is the slower frontier: Chinese brands crossed the [4% market share line in March 2026](#bib-acea), and the planned Spanish, Hungarian and [Turkish plants](#bib-byd-manisa) are the response. Southeast Asia is further along, with Chinese OEMs reaching about [15.7% of the ASEAN-6 market](#bib-aaf) in the first half of 2026, which explains the two Thai plants and the two Indonesian ones. Brazil is where the story is loudest: electrified vehicles climbed to **[25.2% of the car market](#bib-anfavea)** in 2026, a share change European policy debate still tends to project forward rather than report back. The US sits outside this map; tariffs on Chinese finished vehicles have kept the share effectively at zero, which is why no Chinese OEM has announced a US assembly plant and Geely's Ridgeville line continues to be its only US-flagged capacity.

## 4. The quiet constraint is the mineral chain, not the plant

A regional EV plant is a bet on being able to source cathodes, anodes, magnets and power electronics within a reasonable supply radius. On the current data, that bet still points back to China for most of the stages that matter. Our [Mineral Flows Map](/mineral-flows) shows roughly [95% of LFP cathode manufacturing](/mineral-flows-research#fact-14) sitting inside China ([IEA](#bib-iea-gcmo), [Benchmark](#bib-benchmark)), the chemistry the Camaçari and Rayong lines mostly build on, and the chain-diagram view for nickel ends in Chinese cathode manufacturing even when the ore is Indonesian or Brazilian. A [Hungarian plant with LFP cells](#bib-byd-szeged) does not escape that geometry; neither does a [Thai one](#bib-byd-rayong). The companion [research paper](/mineral-flows-research) walks through why.

That is the point industrial-policy conversations in Brussels, Brasília and Jakarta have begun to focus on. A plant on your soil is one thing. The supply pipe behind it is another. The Hungarian and Spanish plants will reopen the European argument about whether building the cell plant is enough, or whether the cathode-active-material step needs to come too; the Brazilian and Indonesian plants will reopen the same argument on the ore end, which is where [Africa's and South America's reserves](/mineral-flows-research#method-reserves) now re-enter the frame.

## What this piece does not say

Three things that would need a separate study.

First, no claim about profitability. Capacity utilisation at Rayong currently runs around 30%, which is a story about demand ramp and local-content rules, not a story about Chinese OEMs being unable to make money in Thailand. We report it; we do not infer from it.

Second, no claim about job creation net of displacement. The Camaçari and Iracemápolis sites were idle or closing before the Chinese acquisitions, so a job gained at a reopened plant is not a like-for-like replacement of a job lost at the same site. Independent impact tracking of the kind the Lab builds for [European funders](/european-impact-tracking) would be the right instrument here.

Third, no projection. Manisa in Turkey is listed as on hold; Lembah Beringin in Malaysia will finish in 2027; the Galicia start is slated for 2028. These are announcements, not deliveries. We report the planning status and leave the forward curve to the companies to defend.
