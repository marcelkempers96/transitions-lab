# Where We Work

*The Lab works where it has genuine roots: real relationships, language, and an understanding of context that cannot be improvised. Three regions, and the thread that connects them.*

Good field research cannot be done at a distance. It depends on relationships that already exist, on language, and on an understanding of a place that takes years to build and cannot be faked in a proposal. The Lab works in the regions where it holds those things genuinely, and is honest about where it does not.

Three regions anchor the work, connected by a single thread: each is a place where technology is meeting people quickly, where the questions are real, and where independent, field-based evidence is scarce.

---

**§ 1 / East Africa**

## Kenya and the wider region

East Africa is where several of the Lab's deepest field relationships sit. Nairobi in particular is a global proving ground for technologies that reach people the incumbent systems never served: mobile money, electric two- and three-wheelers, off-grid energy, and open water and environmental data.

The Lab's field engagements with an electric-mobility manufacturer and with a water-transparency foundation are rooted here, and the region is where the Lab is strongest on the ground: able to field trained researchers who know the context and the language, through standing relationships rather than a flight and a fixer.

*Connected work:* the [Electric Mobility programme](https://www.transitionslab.org/electrification.html), the [Water Access & Transparency programme](https://www.transitionslab.org/water.html), and the [Roam](https://www.transitionslab.org/case-roam.html) and [MiMaji](https://www.transitionslab.org/case-mimaji.html) cases.

**§ 2 / Coastal Southeast Asia**

## Indonesia and the Lombok region

Coastal Indonesia is where the Lab studies technologies at the meeting point of livelihood and ecosystem: biochar and circular systems on smallholder farms, and community-led marine monitoring on the reefs that coastal communities depend on.

These are socio-technical questions in their sharpest form, where a technology's success depends entirely on whether a farmer or a fisher adopts, trusts, and benefits from it. The Lab's engagements with a biochar venture and a marine-monitoring venture are rooted in this region.

*Connected work:* the [Regenerative Agriculture programme](https://www.transitionslab.org/agriculture-programme.html), the [Environmental Monitoring programme](https://www.transitionslab.org/monitoring.html), and the [Pyropower](https://www.transitionslab.org/case-pyropower.html) and [Reef Support](https://www.transitionslab.org/case-reef-support.html) cases.

**§ 3 / The Dutch Caribbean, Suriname and Guyana**

## A distinctive practice

This is the Lab's most distinctive geography, and one very few independent research organisations can credibly serve. The Dutch Caribbean special municipalities, together with Suriname and Guyana, form a region where the Lab holds a rare combination: Dutch-language capacity, familiarity with the institutional context, and water expertise rooted in Delft.

Work here flows through Dutch ministries, Invest International, regional utilities, and the development banks. It is a small field with very few credible, independent, locally-fluent research suppliers, which is precisely why it is a named practice area for the Lab rather than an afterthought. The public-sector and small-island questions here, where imported mainland tools routinely fail, are exactly the kind the Lab is built to answer.

*Connected work:* the [St. Eustatius Transport Directorate](https://www.transitionslab.org/case-statia.html) case, and the Lab's water expertise.

**§ 4 / The thread**

## Rooted, not parachuted

What connects the three regions is not a market map; it is a way of working. In each, the Lab leads with genuine local presence and relationships, pairs them with technical understanding, and reports independently. The Lab does not claim to work everywhere. It claims to work, properly, in the places where it has earned the standing to, and that honesty is part of what makes the evidence worth having.

For funders and partners who increasingly and rightly ask about rootedness, this is the answer: the Lab works where it is rooted, with named local partners, and says so plainly.
