# Transitions Lab — Illustration Brief

**Purpose.** This document lists specific illustration ideas for every published insight article on transitionslab.org, in a form that can be pasted directly into ChatGPT (or another image tool) to generate the artwork. Each item has a title, a placement hint, a type (chart, diagram, or photorealistic scene) and a self-contained prompt.

**How to use.** Copy the "Prompt" block into ChatGPT. The style-guide paragraph at the top of every prompt keeps the whole set visually consistent. Adjust colours, framing or emphasis as you go; the specifics inside each prompt are what matter.

---

## 1. Global style guide

Two visual registers are used across the site. Every prompt below picks one.

### Register A — Line-art title cards and diagrams

Warm cream paper background, colour value roughly `#F5EDDD`. Minimalist editorial line-art at medium weight, black or ink `#1A1A1A` line, no gradients, no drop shadows. Muted accent colours pulled from a fixed palette: coral `#E8642C`, cobalt `#1E3A5F`, butter `#F3DC94`, forest `#1E4032`, plum `#6B2E4B`, sky `#6BA8C4`. Objects sit on a subtle ground line. Composition reads left to right as a small sequence, three to six objects, arrows dashed and hand-drawn. No text inside the image except a title at the top of a title card. Feel: a printed think-tank report from the 1970s, redrawn cleanly today.

### Register B — Photorealistic editorial scenes

Warm, low-saturation photography. Colour palette in the same family — muted cream light, coral and butter accents, cobalt and forest shadows. Editorial framing, human scale, no logos, no brand identifiers, no burnt-in text. Composition avoids clichés: no hard-hat close-ups, no glossy renderings, no drone shots unless a landscape argument requires one. Feel: a Financial Times or Rest of World long-read. If a person is depicted, they should read as a specific person doing specific work, not a stock model.

### Chart aesthetic (where a chart is requested)

Serif or humanist-sans title above the plot area. Two-colour value palette: one accent (usually coral) for the argument's focus and one neutral (usually cobalt or sky) for context. Small footnote in grey at bottom-left. Grid line weight thin, axes labelled sparingly, values placed inside or beside bars rather than on a separate legend when possible. Feel: FT Alphaville, IEEE Spectrum, Our World in Data.

### What not to do

No stock icons, no clip-art vector packs, no isometric 3-D, no neon glow, no futurism tropes (holograms, glass panels, HUDs). No text captions inside the image other than a title on a title card. No people looking directly at the camera. No AI-generated artefacts pretending to be photographs of real named individuals.

## 3. How to place the outputs on the site

1. Save each generated illustration at 1400 × 933 (or the chart's native aspect) as a JPEG at q88 for line-art heroes, or PNG for charts with fine lines.
2. Filename convention: `insight-<slug>-<short-name>.jpg`, kept next to the hero at `/home/user/transitions-lab/assets/img/`.
3. Insert into the article as a `<figure>` block with a real alt-text description of what the image shows, and a figcaption that says what the reader should take away from it, not what the image is a picture of.
4. Regenerate OG cards after any hero change (the existing `generate_og_images.py` script picks up every diagram in `assets/img/` that matches `insight-*-diagram.jpg`).

## 4. Notes for the illustrator

- Wherever the article contains a specific number, the illustration must show that number, not a decorative approximation.
- Wherever an argument turns on a mechanism, the illustration should show the mechanism as steps, not as a metaphor.
- Where a photorealistic option is offered, ChatGPT/Sora may struggle with technical accuracy (specific ports, specific mines). Prefer the line-art register for anything site-specific or contested.
- Every prompt above deliberately avoids naming individuals, brands or logos in the visible output. Keep it that way.
