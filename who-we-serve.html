Who We Serve

The Lab works for anyone who needs honest, independent evidence of what a technology or programme actually does to real people. Five kinds of organisation come to us most often. Find the one that fits, and the door it opens.

Different organisations arrive at the Lab with different questions, but the underlying need is the same: to know what is really happening on the ground, from an independent party with no stake in the answer. What changes is the decision that evidence has to serve. Below, the five audiences we work with most, and what the Lab does for each.

§ 1 / Companies and innovators

For companies moving into a new market

The problem. You have a product that works, and now you need to know how it lands somewhere new, another region, another country, another kind of user, before you commit to scaling. General satisfaction scores will not tell you who adopts, who does not, and why. The risk is spending a scaling budget on an assumption.

What the Lab does. We provide the field evidence that turns an expansion decision into a reasoned one: who actually adopts and who is left out, what the real barriers are (cost, trust, habit, infrastructure), and what would have to change for adoption to hold. We read your strategy through the BRW framework to show which barriers you are bypassing, repurposing, or weakening, and where the fit breaks down.

Proof. The Lab's field engagement with an electric-mobility manufacturer in Nairobi is exactly this kind of work: see the Roam case study.

§ 2 / Funders and public bodies

For funders who need evidence that survives review

The problem. You fund a portfolio, and you need to know which investments are actually working, in a form that travels across very different projects and holds up when someone scrutinises it. Self-reported success from the projects themselves is not enough, and rarely comparable.

What the Lab does. We measure what a programme actually changes, for whom, and through what pathway, against the internationally standard evaluation criteria, so the evidence is defensible and comparable across a portfolio. Because our instruments are standardised, a one-off assessment becomes a trajectory you can manage over time. Independence is the whole point: we have no incentive to find success where there is none.

Proof. See the Lab's Impact Measurement approach and its measurement method in full.

→ Impact Measurement

§ 3 / European projects and consortia

For consortia that need an independent measurement partner

The problem. Your Grant Agreement commits the consortium to monitoring, evaluation, and a proper dissemination and exploitation plan, and the funder expects reach, uptake, and proof that what was promised was delivered. It is easy to set up channels and hard to show they reached anyone. Done late, it becomes a scramble at the final review.

What the Lab does. We act as the independent measurement and dissemination partner: a theory of change and indicator framework set up at the start, a baseline captured before the work begins, continuous monitoring and midline reads while it runs, and an endline evaluation with dissemination that genuinely reaches its audiences, all mapped to periodic reporting. We keep communication, dissemination, and exploitation cleanly separated, the way evaluators expect.

Proof. See the Lab's dedicated European Impact Tracking service and its wider Monitoring, Evaluation & Dissemination offer.

→ European Impact Tracking

§ 4 / NGOs and programmes

For programmes that need honest evidence of what is changing

The problem. You run a programme on the ground and you need to know, truthfully, whether it is working and for whom, not a report written to reassure a board or a donor. You need someone who can reach the people you serve, in their language and their context, and report what they actually find.

What the Lab does. We run rigorous primary research in exactly the settings where it is hardest to do well: low-connectivity, multilingual, informal. We read impact across reach, depth, and experience, so the finding is not just how many were touched, but how materially things changed and what the change feels like to the people inside it, including who was left out.

Proof. See how the Lab works in the field: Field Research, and the Reef Support case study on community-led monitoring.

→ Field Research

§ 5 / Researchers and academic teams

For research teams that need a field partner with reach

The problem. You have a strong research design and need a partner who can carry it into difficult field contexts with real technical understanding of the systems being studied, not a survey firm that treats the technology as a black box.

What the Lab does. We bring social-science fieldcraft and genuine technical literacy to the same study: trained researchers who know the place and the language, paired with people who understand how the systems actually work. We can run the fieldwork, design the instruments, or co-author the analysis, and we hold the work to a standard that survives peer scrutiny.

Proof. See the Lab's field methods and its published frameworks and resources.

§ 6 / And the public interest

The work the Lab does for its own reasons

Beyond commissioned work, the Lab publishes its own research openly, because some questions deserve answering whether or not a client is paying for it. That published work, on electric mobility, regenerative agriculture, environmental monitoring, water access, and finance, is how the Lab serves the wider public interest, and how it keeps its methods sharp. See the research programmes.
