Who We Serve

Five kinds of organisation come to the Lab, each with a different decision to make. What holds them together is the same underlying ask: an independent read of what a technology or programme actually does to real people.

Different organisations arrive with different questions. What changes is the decision the evidence has to serve.

01 · Companies & innovators

For companies moving into a new market

The problem. You have a product that works, and now you need to know how it lands somewhere new. Another region, another country, another kind of user, before you commit to scaling. General satisfaction scores will not tell you who adopts, who does not, and why. The risk is spending a scaling budget on an assumption.

What the Lab does. Field evidence that turns an expansion decision into a reasoned one: who actually adopts and who is left out, what the real barriers are (cost, trust, habit, infrastructure), and what would have to change for adoption to hold. We read your strategy through the BRW framework to show which barriers you are bypassing, repurposing, or weakening, and where the fit breaks down.

Proof. The Lab's field engagement with an electric-mobility manufacturer in Nairobi is exactly this shape of work. See the Roam case study.

02 · Funders & public bodies

For funders who need evidence that survives review

The problem. You fund a portfolio, and you need to know which investments are actually working, in a form that travels across very different projects and holds up when someone scrutinises it. Self-reported success from the projects themselves is not enough, and rarely comparable.

What the Lab does. We measure what a programme actually changes, for whom, and through what pathway, against internationally standard evaluation criteria, so the evidence is defensible and comparable across a portfolio. Because our instruments are standardised, a one-off assessment becomes a trajectory you can manage over time. Independence is the whole point: we have no incentive to find success where there is none.

Proof. See the Lab's Impact Measurement approach and its measurement methods in full.

03 · European projects & consortia

For consortia that need an independent measurement partner

The problem. Your Grant Agreement commits the consortium to monitoring, evaluation, and a proper dissemination and exploitation plan, and the funder expects reach, uptake, and proof that what was promised was delivered. It is easy to set up channels and hard to show they reached anyone. Done late, it becomes a scramble at the final review.

What the Lab does. We act as the independent measurement and dissemination partner: a theory of change and indicator framework set up at the start, a baseline captured before the work begins, continuous monitoring and midline reads while it runs, and an endline evaluation with dissemination that genuinely reaches its audiences, all mapped to periodic reporting.

Proof. See the Lab's dedicated European Impact Tracking service and its wider Monitoring, Evaluation & Dissemination offer.

04 · NGOs & programmes

For programmes that need honest evidence of what is changing

The problem. You run a programme on the ground and you need to know, truthfully, whether it is working and for whom, not a report written to reassure a board or a donor. You need someone who can reach the people you serve, in their language and their context, and report what they actually find.

What the Lab does. Rigorous primary research in the settings where it is hardest to do well: low-connectivity, multilingual, informal. We read impact across reach, depth, and experience, so the finding is not just how many were touched, but how materially things changed and what the change feels like to the people inside it, including who was left out.

Proof. See how the Lab works in the field: Field Research, and the MiMaji case study on community-facing water transparency in Nairobi.

05 · Researchers & academic teams

For research teams that need a field partner with reach

The problem. You have a strong research design and need a partner who can carry it into difficult field contexts with real technical understanding of the systems being studied, not a survey firm that treats the technology as a black box.

What the Lab does. Social-science fieldcraft and genuine technical literacy in the same study. Trained researchers who know the place and the language, paired with people who understand how the systems actually work. We can run the fieldwork, design the instruments, or co-author the analysis, and we hold the work to a standard that survives peer scrutiny.

Proof. See the Lab's field methods and its published frameworks and resources.
