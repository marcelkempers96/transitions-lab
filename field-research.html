Method behind Measuring Change

Field Research

Rigorous primary research in the places it is hardest to do well, built around direct contact with the people a transition affects.

The most important evidence about a technology is held by the people living inside it: the rider financing an electric motorcycle, the farmer deciding whether the kiln is worth the labour, the ranger who knows the reef better than any satellite. The Lab's field research exists to reach that evidence, systematically, ethically, and to a standard that holds up. It is the capability behind our own published research, made available to others.

Where the evidence is hardest to gather, and matters most

The Lab works in settings that defeat conventional research: low-connectivity, multilingual, informal, dispersed. These are also the settings where bad evidence does the most damage. Running research well here demands researchers who speak the language, know the place, and understand the working lives of the people they talk to.

Grounded in real engagements: electric transport in Nairobi (Kenyan e-mobility case), decentralised clean energy in Lombok (Pyropower), water transparency in Nairobi (MiMaji), and five years of sustained fieldwork in Kenya. We do not parachute in. We work through people already part of the context.

Mixed-method research, run properly

What we bring
  • Interviews and qualitative fieldwork. Structured, in-depth conversations that reach past what happened to why. See the interview guide.
  • Surveys at scale. Standardised, repeatable, by phone or face-to-face. Short enough to re-run; rich enough to move a decision.
  • Baseline, midline, endline. The before-and-after spine of any impact claim. Uses the impact-tracking template.
  • Observation and on-site work. Some things are only visible in the doing.

Most research fails in one of two directions: too elaborate to repeat affordably, or too thin to tell you anything new. Our standard is the middle: structured enough to compare, rich enough to matter.

Ethics and rigour are not optional extras

Informed, recorded, revocable consent. Anonymisation by default. Sampling, translation, and quality control managed end to end. We interview to understand, not to confirm.

Ethics standards we work to

Consent, anonymity, and independent review

Every study passes an internal ethics check against these references:

  • The Belmont Report on respect for persons, beneficence, and justice in human-subjects research.
  • WMA Declaration of Helsinki on informed consent.
  • Innovations for Poverty Action and J-PAL on data integrity and IRB oversight.
  • EU GDPR on personal-data handling in every European engagement.
  • ESRC Framework for Research Ethics on qualitative fieldwork.

Commissioned work belongs to the client and stays private unless the client chooses to publish. The case studies on this site are those exceptions.
