Field Research

Primary research in the settings where it is hardest to do well, built around direct contact with the people a transition affects.

The evidence that decides whether a technology works is held by the people inside it: the rider financing an electric motorcycle, the farmer weighing the labour of a new kiln, the ranger who reads the reef better than any satellite. Field research is how we reach that evidence. It is the capability behind our own published research, made available to others.

Where we work

Low-connectivity, multilingual, informal, dispersed. The settings that defeat conventional research.

We work through researchers already part of the context. No parachuting in. Recent ground: electric transport in Nairobi, decentralised clean energy in Lombok, water transparency in Nairobi.

What we bring

  • In-depth interviews. Structured conversations that reach past what happened to why. See the interview guide.
  • Surveys at scale. Standardised, repeatable, by phone or face-to-face. Short enough to re-run; rich enough to move a decision.
  • Baseline, midline, endline. The before-and-after spine of any impact claim. Uses the impact-tracking template.
  • Observation and on-site work. Some things are only visible in the doing.

Structured enough to compare. Rich enough to matter.

Ethics and rigour

Informed, recorded, revocable consent. Anonymisation by default. Sampling, translation and quality control managed end to end. We interview to understand, not to confirm.

Every study is held to the Belmont Report, the WMA Declaration of Helsinki, IPA and J-PAL, EU GDPR, and the ESRC Framework for Research Ethics. See Research Ethics.

Commissioned work belongs to the client and stays private unless they choose to publish. The case studies here are the exceptions.
