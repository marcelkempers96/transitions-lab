Entering a New Context

Field evidence for the decision to enter a new market, launch a new product line, or extend a working model into a new geography.

The cost of being wrong is a scaling budget. The cost of being right is a scoping conversation. Stated demand is a poor guide before capital is committed, so this service reaches the people whose behaviour will decide the outcome and reports what they actually do, want and can afford.

Read the underlying methods Research & Development Support → Market & Expansion Research →

When to use it

  • A working model in one country or city; deciding whether to open a second.
  • A funder or board expects an evidence-based go/no-go before scaling capital.
  • Early sales are ambiguous - real demand, or the founder's network?
  • The new market's payment, regulatory or logistics stack breaks the assumptions from home.
  • You need to know who will and won't adopt, in the segment terms the local reality actually uses.

What the study answers

Four questions recur across every entry study.

01 · Demand, real vs stated

Who says they want it, who has tried to pay for it, and what the walk-away price actually is.

02 · Local segments

The segmentation from your home market usually breaks. We build the new one from the interviews, not the deck.

03 · The stack that carries adoption

Payment, distribution, after-sales, regulation, informal alternatives, trust intermediaries. Any one missing is enough to stall the launch.

04 · Failure modes, named early

The ways this expansion is most likely to fail, ranked. A pre-mortem grounded in field evidence beats discovering failure modes in production.

How it runs

The five-stage method every Lab engagement uses, compressed to a decision timeline:

  1. Scope. The exact go/no-go the study is meant to inform, the segments it must reach, and the evidence that would genuinely change the decision.
  2. Design. A short repeatable survey on demand and price, paired with in-depth interviews. Grounded in the interview guide.
  3. Collect. Local researchers who speak the language and know the market. No parachuting in.
  4. Analyse & benchmark. Against comparable data where it exists, so a number carries its comparison. Failure modes named explicitly.
  5. Deliver. A decision-maker brief, a segment-level read of where adoption is real and where it is fragile, and the instrument in a form that can be re-run.

Turnaround in weeks. Where the answer is "not yet, and here's why," we say so - the point of asking an independent team.
