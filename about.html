About the Lab

An independent research team that studies how technologies meet real people, and turns what it finds into evidence institutions and innovators can act on.

What we believe

The place is the whole argument

A technology only matters when it meets a person, in a place. Not the specification, not the pilot deck, not the aggregate figure. The daily arithmetic of the household, the shift, the field, the shop counter. That is where the Lab starts, and where it reports back to.

Two modes, one method

We publish our own research openly: articles on finance, electrification, and water access, and a signature typology of how new infrastructure engages the legacy regimes it enters.

We work under contract for organisations that need evidence they cannot generate themselves: field research, impact measurement, and the European projects that need an independent measurement partner.

Because we are independent of what we measure, we report what the evidence says. Including when it is inconvenient.

What makes us different

Our stance

Social science first, technical literacy alongside

Two things that rarely sit in the same room. Social-science fieldcraft that finds out what is actually happening to people. And genuine technical understanding of the systems we study. Policy consultancies hold only the first; technical labs, only the second. The Lab refuses the split.

Where we work

Based in Delft, The Netherlands. We do fieldwork in the contexts that defeat conventional research: low-connectivity, multilingual, informal, dispersed. We do not parachute in. We work through researchers and partners who already speak the language and know the place.

How we hold the work

Informed, recorded, revocable consent. Anonymisation by default. We interview to understand, not to confirm, and we report the findings that contradict a client's hopes as carefully as the ones that support them. See Field Research for the full ethics practice.
